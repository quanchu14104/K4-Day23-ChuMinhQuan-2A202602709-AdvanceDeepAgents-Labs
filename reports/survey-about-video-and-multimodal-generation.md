# Survey of Video and Multimodal Generation

**Học viên:** Chu Minh Quân | **MSSV:** 2A202602709

## TL;DR
This report provides a comprehensive overview of video and multimodal generation, covering foundational concepts, core methodologies, empirical benchmarks, and key representative systems. It highlights the advancements, challenges, and future directions in the field, emphasizing the integration of various data types for cross-modal understanding and generation.

## Background & Motivation
Video and multimodal generation have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to integrate various data types for cross-modal understanding and generation. This field addresses challenges in scalability, robustness, and ethical considerations, while also highlighting the complementary strengths of vision-language models and video generation models in spatial intelligence tasks [1][2][3][4][5].

## Core Methodologies
The core methodologies in video and multimodal generation include OmniWeaving, MMVID, and HuMo. OmniWeaving is an omni-level video generation model featuring powerful multimodal composition and reasoning-informed capabilities [6]. MMVID is a multimodal video generation framework that benefits from text and images provided jointly or separately [7]. HuMo is a unified, human-centric video generation framework designed to produce high-quality, fine-grained, and controllable human videos from multimodal inputs [8].

## Empirical Benchmarks
Empirical benchmarks such as T2V-CompBench, UniVBench, Video-Bench, VBench++, and VABench provide comprehensive evaluations of video generation models. These benchmarks cover various aspects of video quality, consistency, and human preferences, offering insights into model performance and future research directions [9][10][11][12][13].

## Trends and Open Problems
The field of video and multimodal generation is rapidly evolving, with ongoing research focused on improving model performance, addressing ethical considerations, and developing comprehensive benchmarks. Future directions include the integration of advanced algorithms, the exploration of new training paradigms, and the establishment of standardized evaluation frameworks [14][15][16][17].
